# The outside-in audit — no Studio access needed

`deliverables/channel-audit-template.md` is the paid audit you run once someone gives you Studio
access. This is the other one: what you send a prospect who has not replied yet, or has not said
yes yet. Built entirely from what is public on the channel page.

Roughly 40 minutes per channel. It is the thing to send Hannes.

---

## Why this version exists

Hannes never gave you access, and he is not going to before he trusts you. The free-audit offer
you already made him twice needs something attached to it or it stays an offer. An audit that
needs nothing from him can be sent today, unprompted.

It also inverts the ask. Instead of "give me a call and I will look at your channel," it becomes
"I already looked, here are three things, the fourth needs your analytics." That last clause is
the call booking, and it is doing the work that a request never does.

## Part A — the 40-minute method

Everything here is visible logged out. Open the channel, Videos tab, sort by Popular, then again
by Latest. Two tabs, side by side.

### A1. The outlier read (do this first, it is the whole audit)

Write down the view count of the last 15 long-form videos. Find the median. Now find anything
sitting at 3x the median or higher.

That video is the answer to what the audience actually wants, and almost nobody acts on their
own outlier. They post it, it does well, and then they go back to the schedule.

For each outlier ask: what is different? Topic, title shape, guest, format, length, thumbnail.
Usually one variable. Name it.

**This single section is worth the whole audit.** It is concrete, it is flattering (their best
work), and it points at an obvious action.

If nothing is at 3x, that is itself the finding: a flat view distribution on a small channel
means nothing has broken out yet, and the topics are all sitting in the same narrow band.

### A2. Titles, read as promises

List the last ten titles. For each, one word: what does the viewer get if they click?

The failure mode you will see most often on agency and founder channels is titles that describe
rather than promise. "Our work with Acme Corp" describes. "How Acme went from 40 leads a month
to 300" promises. Nobody searches a client's name.

Count how many of the ten name the viewer's problem versus the creator's activity. That ratio
goes in the report.

### A3. Thumbnails at phone size

Screenshot the video grid, then shrink it to about 25% and look again. That is roughly how it
appears in a feed on a phone.

Check: how many words on each, is the face readable, do three thumbnails in a row look
identical, does any single one say something different from its title. Title and thumbnail
saying the same thing twice is a wasted slot. They should be two halves of one idea.

### A4. Cadence

Note the gaps between uploads over the last three months. Irregular posting means YouTube never
learns when to push. A four-week gap after a good video is the most expensive mistake on a small
channel, because the momentum from the outlier gets thrown away.

### A5. The funnel, all of it public

This is your section and the one nobody else writes. Walk it as a viewer:

1. Description box: is there a link in the first two lines, before the fold?
2. Where does the link go? Homepage, or the thing the video was about?
3. Is there any step between the click and a paid ask, or does a stranger land on a price?
4. Pinned comment: used, or empty?
5. End screen and channel trailer: pointing anywhere useful?
6. Is there anything at all that captures an email from someone not ready to buy?

Every gap here is a leak, and you can name all of them without ever logging in.

### A6. Comments as a demand signal

Read the comments on the two best-performing videos. You are looking for buying questions ("do
you work with X", "what would this cost", "can you do this for") versus compliments.

Buying questions in the comments with no CTA in the description is the cleanest possible finding.
It means demand is arriving and nothing is there to catch it.

### A7. What you cannot see, and say so

You cannot see CTR, impressions, retention, or traffic sources from outside. Do not pretend.
Naming the limit is what makes the rest credible, and it is the natural bridge to the paid work.

## Part B — the send-ready report

Two pages. Strip the bracketed instructions before sending.

---

**[Channel name] — outside-in review**
Flavio M. · [date]

I said I would do this, so here it is. No access to your analytics, so this is everything
readable from the outside. Three of the four things below are fixable without me.

**What is working**

[Two or three specific things. Name videos by title. This is not politeness, it is proof you
actually watched. If you bring only problems you read as a critic, and nobody hires a critic to
run their channel.]

**Your best video is telling you something**

[The outlier from A1. Name it, give the view count against the median, and name the one variable
that was different. Then the obvious action: more of that variable.]

[If there is no outlier: "Nothing has broken out yet. Your views sit in a narrow band, which
usually means the topics are all the same distance from what people are searching for."]

**Your titles describe, they do not promise**

[The ratio from A2. Then two or three of their real titles rewritten as promises. Actual
rewrites, not advice about rewriting. This is the part people screenshot.]

**Where the leads are leaking**

[The funnel walk from A5, as a numbered path with the break named at each step. If A6 turned up
buying questions with no CTA, lead with that, it is the strongest single line in the report.]

**Three I would test**

[Three concrete titles, tied to who clicks and why that person is worth having. Not themes.]

**What I would need to go further**

Three things are invisible from out here: your click-through rate, where viewers drop off, and
where the traffic is actually coming from. Those decide whether this is a packaging problem or a
retention one, and they point at opposite fixes.

Twenty minutes in your analytics and I would know which.

Free for a call this week?

Flavio

## Hannes specifically

From `outreach/youtube-hannes-media-agency.md`: marketing agency, channel is case studies and
success stories, and the goal he stated was clients.

Three hypotheses to check first. Written as hypotheses because you have not opened the channel
yet, and they are not findings until you have.

1. **Case studies titled by client name.** The classic agency-channel failure. "How we helped
   [Client]" is a title only the client searches. Rewritten toward the outcome and the problem,
   the same footage reaches people with that problem. If this is true it is your headline finding
   and it is a same-day fix.
2. **The audience is peers, not buyers.** Agency channels drift into content that other agency
   people enjoy, because those are the people who engage. Check the comments. If it is mostly
   marketers complimenting the work rather than business owners asking questions, the channel is
   building the wrong audience and views will keep looking fine.
3. **No capture step.** Case studies build trust and then send viewers nowhere. Check whether
   the description does anything before the fold.

Do not write these into the report as facts. Open the channel, confirm or kill each one, and
report what you actually find.
