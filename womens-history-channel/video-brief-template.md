# Video Brief Template — Flavio's daily 1-2 hour output

One of these per day = the whole job. Copy this block into `video-ideas-log.md` under the next
video number, fill it in, ping Rishabh. Do not write the script yourself — that's his job, and
doing it for him defeats the point of splitting the work.

```
## Video [N]: [working title]

**Template:** A (biography) or B (listicle erasure)
**Real name/case:** [who this is actually about — replace any bracket placeholder from the
  roadmap's draft 15 with the real person/event you researched]

**Why this one, one line:** [what makes it dark/injustice-led rather than plain-admiring —
  this is the thing that separates working channels from the two failed ones]

**Comparable videos (2-3):** [pull from roadmap.md — which working channel has done something
  close to this, and what specifically worked in their version: the angle, the twist, the
  pacing. If you can grab a transcript or watch a similar video, note the actual beat that
  hooked you, not just "it did well"]

**Storyboard (beats, not script):**
1. Hook (0:00-0:30) — [the disturbing/unfair fact that opens it]
2. [Beat 2]
3. [Beat 3]
4. [Beat 4]
5. [Beat 5, optional]
6. Close — [the erased/injustice payoff, the line that makes someone comment]

**Flags for Rishabh:** [anything unverified, any fact you're not sure holds up, any sourcing gap]
```

## Why this shape and not a full script

Flavio's edge is finding the case and seeing the shape of the story, fast, from having looked at
what 27 channels actually did. Rishabh's edge is turning a shape into 1,500 words that hold
someone's attention for 12-15 minutes. Handing him a full script wastes the split; handing him
five bullet points wastes his time re-deriving the story. A storyboard is the right altitude.

## Time budget inside the 1-2 hours

- 20-30 min: pick/confirm the real name or case for the next title on the backlog
- 30-45 min: pull 2-3 comparable videos, watch enough to name the specific hook/beat that worked
- 20-30 min: write the beats and flag anything unverified

If a case falls apart under research (not enough sourced material, or the "erased" framing
doesn't actually hold up), drop it and move to the next title on the backlog rather than forcing
it — a thin brief produces a thin script.
