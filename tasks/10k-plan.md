# The path to $10K a month

Written 14 Aug 2026. Your ask: win clients, then hire, then agency, $10K USD/month.

The sequence is right. Two things in it need correcting, and one piece of math decides
everything else.

---

## 1. The math that settles it

Hourly cannot get you to $10K. Not at any realistic rate.

| Route | Rate | Hours needed for $10K/mo |
|---|---|---|
| Upwork hourly | $18.50 | 135 hrs/week |
| Upwork hourly | $35 | 71 hrs/week |
| Upwork hourly | $62 | 40 hrs/week, every week, forever |

At $18.50 across a full 40 hour week you land near $2,900 after Upwork's cut. That is a good
number and it is your 90 day target from context.md. It is not this target.

So Upwork is not the income. **Upwork is the client acquisition channel for the agency.** That
reframe is the whole plan. Every bid you send is prospecting, and the contract is the first
transaction in a relationship priced somewhere else.

## 2. Retainer math, and why fewer clients beats more

$1,500/month retainer, delivery cost roughly $550/channel once you are hiring (editor around
$400 for four long-form, thumbnails around $150).

| Clients | Retainer | Gross | Delivery | Net to you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | $1,500 | $10,500 | $3,850 | $6,650 |
| 5 | $2,500 | $12,500 | $2,750 | $9,750 |
| 4 | $3,000 | $12,000 | $2,200 | $9,800 |

Seven clients at $1,500 does not get you there. Four at $3,000 does, with less delivery load and
fewer people to manage.

**So the job is not "get more clients." It is "be able to charge $3,000."** Those are completely
different problems. More clients is a hustle problem. Higher price is a proof problem.

What moves you from $1,500 to $3,000 is one thing: being able to say what your work did to
revenue, not what it did to views. That is why today's proposal leads with a channel that got
sold and 3,000 dollars a month in ad revenue, and why the honest line about not being the closer
matters more than it costs. Clients pay a premium for someone who does not inflate.

## 3. The correction to "win clients then hire"

You have it backwards in one place. Do not wait until you are drowning.

**Hire on client two, not client four.**

- **Client 1: you deliver everything yourself.** Not because it scales, but because it is how
  you find out what the actual steps are. Write them down as you go. That document is the SOP.
- **Client 2: hire one editor and onboard them with client 1's SOP.** This is the real test. If
  someone else can produce acceptable output from your written process, you have a business. If
  they cannot, you have a job with extra steps, and better to learn that at two clients than five.
- **Client 3 and 4: same editor, add a thumbnail designer.** Your time moves to strategy, packaging
  and the client calls. That is the part only you can do and the part they are paying for.
- **Client 5 plus: part-time ops person** to chase footage, schedule uploads and run the calendar.
  This is the hire that buys back your weeks.

If you hire at client four instead of client two, quality drops right when you need reviews most,
and the reviews are the only thing making the next client cheap to win.

Note what BID #1 and #2 in today's inbox actually are. A client paying $75 each for the SOP that
keeps a multi-person editing and thumbnail team consistent. That is someone paying you to write
the document your own agency needs. Take both.

## 4. The funnel

```
Upwork bid  ->  $300-500 audit  ->  audit call  ->  $1,500-3,000/mo retainer
```

Each arrow, and why it holds:

**Bid to audit.** A stranger will not buy a $2,000 retainer. They will buy a $400 audit, because
the risk is nothing. Your bid should often be pitching the audit, not the retainer.

**Audit to call.** Never email the finished audit. Present it live. The delivery call is a warm
sales call where you have already done unpaid-feeling work and found real problems.

**Call to retainer.** This is your unfair advantage and you should stop underrating it. Most
YouTube freelancers cannot sell at all. You have run a thousand plus discovery and demo calls and
closed 250K. The audit exists to manufacture the call, because the call is where you win.

Template and the diagnostic tree: `deliverables/channel-audit-template.md`.

## 7. What would make this fail

Naming these now so they are recognisable later.

- **Charging $1,500 forever.** The table in section 2 is unforgiving about this. Raise on client
  three or you will be running seven channels for six thousand dollars.
- **Hiring late.** Covered in section 3. Client two.
- **Selling retainers cold.** The audit is not an optional nicety, it is the mechanism.
- **Letting delivery eat the selling time.** The week you stop bidding is the week the pipeline
  starts dying, and you find out about it two months later.
- **Quitting the job in phase 1.** Fund the hires. Price from strength.
