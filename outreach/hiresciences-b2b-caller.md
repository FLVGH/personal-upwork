# HireSciences — B2B Caller (machine shops + HVAC)

Invitation. Bucket A (sales/closing). Bid $23/hr, no boost (invitation).

## The call's real job
Not selling the $2,950 build. Get a micro-yes: intake done live on the call ($75)
or book the 15-min walkthrough (fallback). Skill = holding a busy tradesman on
the line long enough to finish the intake right there. Momentum is the game.
Sell the bad-hire burn, not the software. Duck "how much" until the intake.

## 60-second voice opener — "Mike" (record this yourself, don't polish)

Simple words, short questions, let him build the case. Each step earns a small
yes so the ask at the end is just the next small yes. He prices his own bad hire,
so the cost is his number, not my pitch.

> Hey Mike, Flavio from HireScience. Quick one, you got a sec?
>
> [yes] — small commitment, and permission
>
> You're the guy who does the hiring over there, right?
>
> [yes] — confirms decision maker, second yes
>
> How many people you bring on in a year?
>
> [number] — he's engaged, gave me a real figure
>
> And straight up, of those, how many turned out to be the wrong guy?
>
> [number] — he says the pain out loud
>
> Yeah. And one of those, ballpark, what's it run you by the time you know?
> The training, his pay, the jobs he slowed down.
>
> [number] — Hormozi math, he builds the cost himself
>
> Right. So every time you guess wrong off an interview, that's [$X] gone.
>
> [mirror his own number back — now it's his math, not my claim]
>
> That's the whole thing we fix. We build a test for your exact job. Guy does it
> before you hire him, you see what he can actually run. No more guessing.
>
> Worth ten minutes to show you how it'd look for your shop?
>
> [if bad time] No sweat. Fifteen minutes later this week. Mornings or
> afternoons easier for you?

The one line that carries the whole call: "what's it run you by the time you
know?" Everything before it sets up that number. Everything after just points
the fix at it. Don't rush it, let him actually count.

## Alt opener — the "follow-up" angle (harder open, warm list)

Use when the list is people I've already emailed. Opens as a follow-up so it
lands lighter than a cold hit. Same job: walk him into the pain, then the ask.

> Hey Mike, Flavio from HireScience. Been emailing you, figured a call's
> faster. Got twenty seconds?
>
> [yes] — permission
>
> You're the one who handles hiring over there, right?
>
> [yes] — decision maker
>
> When'd you last bring someone on?
>
> [answer] — situation on the table
>
> That one stick? Or did it not pan out?
>
> [if it stuck] Every one of them work out? Or you had a couple you'd
> take back?
>
> [he names a bad hire]
>
> What happened there?
>
> [answer] — pain out loud
>
> And why'd it go that way, you reckon?
>
> [answer] — he diagnoses it himself, not me
>
> So you interview, you check the boxes, and you still end up with the
> wrong guy?
>
> [yes]
>
> Cost you time and money to find that out, right?
>
> [yes] — implication landed
>
> Right. So every wrong read off an interview drags your whole floor back.
> That's the exact thing we fix. Give me eleven minutes and I'll show you how
> we sorted it for a couple shops in your area. Worth a look?
>
> [if bad time] No sweat. Grab fifteen with you later this week. Mornings
> or afternoons easier?

Keep "eleven minutes" odd and specific, it reads real where "ten" reads
scripted. The turn is "that one stick?" It forks smooth-hire and bad-hire into
the same pain without you having to guess which he'll say.

## Objection deflections (anticipate these)
- "How much is it?" -> "Depends on the role, that's exactly what the ten-minute
  intake sorts out. Let's get through that first."
- "I'm busy / not a good time." -> "Totally get it, that's why it's ten minutes,
  not a meeting. Or I grab fifteen with you Thursday. Which is easier?"
- "We handle our own hiring." -> "For sure, this isn't instead of that, it's so
  the guy you pick actually holds up on the floor. Nothing changes on your end."
- "Just send me an email." -> "Happy to, but the emails are what got us here.
  Ten minutes now and it's actually done. You free right this second?"
